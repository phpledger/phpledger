PHP Ledger 0.6.0-preview: a rebuilt accounting workspace

A focused Home, compact registers and reviewed document editors bring daily work and its accounting evidence into the same workspace.

This release rebuilds the PHP-rendered interface with a warm light canvas, navy actions, local Inter fonts, a workspace sidebar and company switcher. It remains a development preview for evaluation with sample data.

What changed in daily work?

  • Home brings quick actions, work needing attention, cash and amounts due together.
  • Search or jump to and quick-create navigation help you reach the next task.
  • Registers use searchable, sortable lists with 25, 50 or 100 rows per page. Filters remain in the URL.
  • Invoice and bill editors start with one line, show exact totals and separate draft saving from posting review.
  • Corrections retain document identity through reviewed reversal and replacement entries.

What changed in accounting review?

One settlement can allocate across multiple open items atomically. Foreign-exchange gain/loss fields are required when applicable. Stock-count and goods-receipt previews, linked purchasing context and supplier return guidance clarify the review steps. Profit and loss adds cost-of-sales classification, gross profit and period presets. Existing unclassified charts preserve their prior net-profit calculations.

What remains unfinished?

Desktop and tablet are the design targets. Mobile refinement and dark mode are deferred. Complete visual and accessibility acceptance, consistent field-level validation recovery and the full nested report/source/action return journey remain unfinished. No WCAG certification, accounting sign-off, country compliance or production-readiness claim is made.

Try the current interface

  1. Choose a fictional business in the demo and review its Home and sample guide.
  2. Open Invoices, inspect a record and start a new draft. Review its lines, tax mode and posting preview.
  3. Open Profit & loss, choose a period and follow an account to its statement.
  4. Compare those screens with your own expected workflow before considering real books.
